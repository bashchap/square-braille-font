# Christmas Snow handover — updated 2026-09-15

This is the zero-context continuation document for the Christmas Snow demo.
Read it with `demos/seasonal/README.md`; the README is the user guide, while
this file records implementation boundaries, verified behaviour, and next
engineering work.

## Current entry points

- Viewer: `demos/seasonal/christmas_snow.py`
- Live TUI: `demos/seasonal/christmas_snow_control.py`
- Regression suite: `demos/seasonal/verify_christmas_snow.py`
- Full-engine performance report: `demos/seasonal/PERFORMANCE-2026-09-15.md`
- Phase benchmark: `demos/seasonal/benchmark_christmas_snow.py`
- macOS launcher: `scripts/macos/run-demo.sh pua4 christmas-snow`
- Optional native analyser: `native/seasonal_encoder/`

The controller is not a viewer demo name. Launch the viewer with
`christmas-snow --listen`, and launch the controller separately with
`christmas-snow-control`; both use the same JSON control file. `S` saves a
preset plus a reproducible command. `X` requests an in-process viewer restart,
preserving the terminal window and applying every current controller value.

## Renderer and font facts

The scene is procedurally rasterized into a virtual-pixel `Surface`. Trees,
cabins, rabbits, people, weather, aircraft, Santa, Superman, UFOs, and the
helicopter are code-generated geometry, not bitmap assets and not dynamically
created fonts. The installed PUA4 font is a static repertoire of 65,536 masks.
One mask is selected per 4x4 cell, with ANSI foreground and sometimes
background colour. The FONT dashboard reports masks used in the current frame
and cumulatively since start; `RUNTIME REDEFINED` and `DYNAMICALLY CREATED`
remain zero.

Static cached geometry currently covers trees and postman poses. Moving
aircraft are inexpensive relative to tree rasterization and cell encoding, so
a large sprite atlas is not yet the best performance target.

## Scene behaviour now implemented

- Pine, fir, spruce, oak, maple and birch use deterministic procedural rules.
  Trunk thickness, independent branch ratio, branch count/levels/angle/length,
  colour separation, sway, lights and segment budget are controlled live.
- Snow has ground/full/none physics modes, slope relaxation, local tower
  collapse, object retention and gravity shedding. A second local mass rule
  starts a repeatable random countdown when `snow-fallaway-threshold` is
  sustained, then releases only the configured `snow-fallaway-width` area.
  The plough follows a fixed
  horizontal datum and no longer erases snowfall deposited behind a completed
  pass. Active collapse footprints reject new deposits and cannot be refilled
  by general slope relaxation, so a shoulder cannot hold the collapse slot open
  indefinitely. The SNOW dashboard exposes broad/mass thresholds, the active
  mass countdown and separate totals.
- Every cabin door is linked to a shared loose-pixel dirt road. The postman
  walks that road, turns toward the selected real door, scales with depth,
  collapses snow under the approach, posts, waits, returns and continues.
- Rabbits have persistent near/middle/far depth and parallax. Existing visible
  rabbits—not manufactured ones—are selected for UFO capture. Rabbit and beam
  retain the same original lane throughout vertical lift.
- NPC ground sampling is non-wrapping and footprint-filtered. Their feet remain
  on the ground side of the artificial horizon even when an extreme snow bank
  rises above it; replacement generations still enter only from configured
  scene boundaries.
- Santa follows an arc and scales from the configured far point to midpoint
  maximum and back. The festive trail persists. Each crossed chimney receives
  one simultaneous group of `santa-presents-min..max` parcels with independent
  initial fall speeds.
- Superman has straight/curve/arc paths and independent frequency/speed. The
  silhouette is horizontally extended, with a smaller fluttering cape, face,
  hair, eye, outstretched fist, suit shading, belt, chest emblem and boots.
- Aeroplanes include commuter and airliner forms, distant pilot ejection, and
  a larger animated parachute. After ejection, the aircraft can spin through a
  configurable toward/away crash arc with fire and smoke into a persistent
  fiery or nuclear/mushroom impact. Kite tail segments respond cumulatively to
  live wind and gusts.
- UFO variants swoop from and return to a point, emit plasma, align vertically
  before capture, and expose one of four sparse animated beam styles only
  while lifting.
- The `helicopter` AH-64 and black/red `airwolf` types share a dedicated
  sequence rather than a horizontal flyby:
  doubled-size detailed frontal approach, horizontally varied hover, descent
  into the foreground, configurable landed wait, crate release, vertical lift
  above the highest cabin silhouette, stationary thirteen-orientation turn,
  then low rear-view recession toward the horizon. The departure continuously
  applies a scale-aware lower-silhouette clearance plane, so a near craft cannot
  shrink across a cabin roof while remaining in the foreground lane.
  Rotor coupling pushes precipitation, emits radial/volumetric particles and
  scours a snow bowl with independently controlled strength and width. The
  yaw renderer projects a longitudinal hull, tandem canopy, separate angular
  engine nacelles, stub-wing stores, sensors, chain gun, landing gear, tail,
  Longbow radar and four thin filled rotor blades. The axial frames explicitly
  restore paired canopy panes, frames, engine mouths and sensors after
  projection; the Airwolf variant swaps in its pointed dark hull, silver glass
  and red stripe. Downwash begins below/outside the fuselage and is composited
  behind it. Landing selection rejects a site occupied by a live rabbit or
  postman, then the hover/descent/landed/takeoff radius excludes rabbits, postman and
  tumbleweed. `render_helicopter_mockup.py` produces either all-angle contact
  sheet through the real PUA4/ANSI quantizer (`--style airwolf` for the second).
- Foreground (`aircraft-crash-depth toward`) impact heat removes bank and
  retained object snow within a tapered radius while the fire lasts. Fiery and
  nuclear impacts use layered red/yellow ground fire, hot cores and embers,
  then progressively darken through the final 38% instead of disappearing.
- A landed crate is not an obstacle. The next postman stops, opens it, takes
  mail, breaks it, and continues to the scheduled cabin; fragments fade.
- Clouds have colour, depth and parallax controls. Rain, hail, blue-edged
  lightning and
  foreground/background weather assignment share the weather tab.
- `horizon-structure` can add seeded dirt, receding huts or both between a
  configurable artificial horizon and the main ground. Trees and cabins use
  ground-contact position to correct overlaps such as an elevated A-frame
  that should remain behind a nearer tree.

## Depth order

The broad order is: sky gradient; far clouds/flights/parachutists/presents;
background precipitation and far rabbits; cached scenery; accumulated snow and
dirt route; landing helicopter/downwash/crates; near rabbits/postman; foreground
precipitation; plough. A helicopter retains its selected far/near perspective
lane for the complete event. A near helicopter therefore stays in front of
scenery, but its takeoff/turn/departure geometry is constrained above cabin
silhouettes before recession begins.

## Native performance phases 1–3

`native/seasonal_encoder` is a no-dependency Rust `cdylib`, loaded once through
`ctypes`. It batch-analyses seasonal cells and exposes shared full terminal
encoders for the older framebuffer demos. `--native-encoder auto` safely falls
back, `off` forces Python, and `on` requires the library. The full verifier
checks exact ANSI and stats parity when the release library exists.

Phase three is complete. `Surface` now stores contiguous packed RGB and
priority planes, and Rust borrows them without rebuilding tuple arrays.
`--native-surface auto|off|on` independently selects batched native rectangles,
lines, thick lines, ellipses, polygons, overlays, priority promotion,
exposed-edge extraction, accumulation and cached-tree compositing. AUTO is the
production default when the native encoder is available. The Python packed
path remains the behavioral and pixel oracle. The verifier compares every
migrated operation byte-for-byte before checking final ANSI and telemetry.
There is no per-pixel FFI boundary.

The shared Rust encoder is integrated into basic geometry/snow/starfield/trail,
vector tunnel/Elite/Defender, Doom, Enterprise/Spaceship, PUA4 motion/Vortex,
Voyager and layered Voyager. Direct cursor/seam probes, catalogues, font probes
and the editor remain Python by design because their output is not a cell
framebuffer. Scene simulation and procedural art also remain Python; a literal
full rewrite of every demo would duplicate behaviour without moving the
measured hot path. Monochrome mode intentionally ignores the supplied colour
payload, including historical callers that still pass RGB tuples; native and
Python fallbacks are regression-tested for this contract.

The 2026-09-16 full-workload benchmark measures 25.12 ms at 303x46 versus the
previous 132.90 ms encoder-only production path: 81.1% lower, or 5.29x faster.
In an isolated pair, the native surface changes a Rust-encoded frame from
105.52 to 23.85 ms. At 120x36, 168x60 and 303x46 the measured
terminal-independent ceilings are 79.60, 48.71 and 39.81 fps respectively on
the development Mac. See the performance
report for exact phase tables and reproduction commands.

NPC navigation also caches the shared perspective-projected cabin path network
and cabin obstacles once per frame. Keep behavioral state machines in Python;
the latest profile provides no basis for moving them. Rust remains preferable
to C (similar ceiling, safer buffer ownership) and Go (less natural in-process
FFI and garbage-collector interaction).

## Performance phase 2: cached-tree compositing

Profiling a 303x46 PUA4, full-physics workload found scenery rebuilding to be
the largest Python raster cost. Cached geometry previously recalculated the
same height-weighted sway clamp, multiply and rounding for every painted tree
pixel. It now builds one displacement per occupied scanline and reuses it.
Against the previous algorithm under the same process and workload, median
scenery time changed from 44.465 to 33.139 ms (25.5% lower); complete frame
time changed from 120.966 to 110.645 ms (8.5% lower). The benchmark also now
selects the loaded Rust encoder just as the live viewer does.

## Performance phase 3: native rendering data plane complete

The 2026-09-15 full workload adds eight NPCs, six rabbits, eight clouds, seven
depth-varied cabins, all scenery/ambient systems, 525 weather particles and a
near detailed helicopter. At 303x46/full physics, Python encoding measured
158.77 ms/frame total and Rust encoding measured 132.90 ms/frame. On the Rust
path, scenery plus raster/compositing was 62.4%, encoding with Python packing
was 30.0%, and simulation plus collision was 7.4%.

That migration is implemented: packed RGB/priority `Surface`, zero-copy cell
analysis, shared batched primitives, accumulation, cached-tree compositing and
once-per-frame NPC cabin-path projection. State machines and procedural art
definitions remain in Python. The detailed measurements, reproduction commands
and parity gates are in `PERFORMANCE-2026-09-15.md`.

## Known limitations and next work

- A terminal is a coarse, colour-stateful output device. A full gradient sky
  increases coloured cell combinations and ANSI output; moving objects can
  therefore look blockier even when depth order is correct.
- Landing-site selection is horizontally varied and avoids live people and
  rabbits, but not every procedurally placed cabin or tree; a future scene
  graph should reserve a static-scenery-clear rotor pad before placement.
- The dirt route is visual and intentionally non-colliding. Snow may partly
  obscure it; this is desirable, but a later footprint/wear field could expose
  the most-used portions dynamically.
- Continue to test the packed/native path on Linux and Windows whenever those
  hosts are available; macOS and the Python/Rust parity suite pass now.
